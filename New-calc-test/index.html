How this projection works · scope and sources
  • Optional Age Pension is an estimate of your own payment from age 67, assuming residence eligibility, standard domestic rates and no unentered assets/income. Both tests are applied using opening-year assessable super, deemed income and entered wages. Your super drawdown is not also counted as income under the modern deeming rules. Partner accumulation super is exempt before 67; a partner pension is assessed earlier. Partner pensions and withdrawals are not included in your personal income target.
  • July 2026 asset free areas: single homeowner $333,000 / non-homeowner $600,000; couple combined $499,000 / $766,000. Reduction per fortnight is $3 per $1,000 for singles, $1.50 for your share as a couple. Income free areas are $226 / $396 combined per fortnight, with reductions of 50c / 25c per excess dollar respectively. Deeming thresholds: $66,800 / $110,600 combined. Current maximum fortnightly payments are $1,200.90 single / $905.20 partnered each, with 1.25% / 3.25% deeming. Announced 20 September 2026 payments ($1,237.70 / $933 each) and deeming (1.75% / 3.75%) are included. First-year amounts are weighted by 81 days before and 284 days after that change. Thereafter rates and thresholds remain fixed nominally; future indexation is not forecast.
  • Income is reassessed annually, not fortnightly. The ordinary $300/fortnight Work Bonus is applied to eligible wages; banked balances are excluded. No Rent Assistance, special/transitional rates, overseas rules, claim delays, personal tax or ongoing accumulation of cash withdrawn outside super. Partner modelling excludes future contributions and fees; use this only as an indicative scenario. Grey means no entitlement estimated in the selected horizon, not a finding of permanent ineligibility.
  • Australian taxed accumulation funds and account-based pensions only. Retirement assumes a valid condition of release from age 60, or unrestricted access at 65. No defined benefits, untaxed schemes, other assets or personal income tax assessment.
  • Standard concessional cap $32,500; after-tax annual cap $130,000; general pension transfer cap $2.1 million. The pension cap limits commencement transfers, not later investment growth within the pension. Caps are held fixed, with no assumed future indexation. Carry-forward and bring-forward arrangements are not modelled. After-tax contributions are omitted when opening total super is at least $2.1 million. Assumes no other super accounts or prior contributions in the modelled financial year.
  • Percentage employer contributions use salary as eligible earnings; dollar employer contributions grow with wages. Employer amounts above the fixed $32,500 concessional cap are paid into super and initially taxed at 15%. The calculator then estimates the additional personal tax on the excess using 2026–27 resident rates plus a full 2% Medicare levy and assumes that tax is released from super. The remaining excess stays in super and counts towards the non-concessional cap. Medicare reductions and exemptions, tax offsets, deductions, other income and carry-forward cap amounts are not modelled.
  • Salary sacrifice and personal deductible contributions use the standard cap space remaining after employer contributions; requested amounts above that space are omitted. Contributions tax is 15%. Division 293 is a salary-only estimate above $250,000, paid from super. Other income, deductions and adjustments may change it. LISTO, government co-contributions and spouse offsets are excluded. Personal deductible contributions assume eligibility and an acknowledged notice of intent. Voluntary contributions are omitted from age 75; birthday-month exceptions are not modelled.
  • Pension minimums use opening pension balance and age: under 65: 4%; 65–74: 5%; 75–79: 6%; 80–84: 7%; 85–89: 9%; 90–94: 11%; 95+: 14%. Rounded to the nearest $10. Full years only; no commencement-year pro-rating. TTR is limited to 10%.
  • Annual returns are compounded monthly. Contributions arrive at the start of each month; earnings, estimated tax, directly deducted administration fees and withdrawals follow. Withdrawals cannot exceed available funds. Retirement income above available pension funds can be drawn from the remaining accessible accumulation account. Any minimum withdrawal above spending needs leaves super; no outside-super reinvestment is modelled.
  • Cbus crediting rates report investment earnings after embedded investment tax, investment fees and transaction costs. The separate investment-tax amount is unavailable from these net rates. Direct administration fees are applied to each active account. Cbus limits certain combined administration and investment fees to 3% when a product balance is under $6,000 at financial year end; an exact refund is not modelled because the investment fee embedded in a crediting rate cannot be isolated. Published historical returns are used as constant projection assumptions, not forecasts.
  • The projection continues through the chosen end age. Where opening or closing balances exceed the fixed $3 million threshold, results exclude Division 296 tax and are not a complete after-tax projection. Affected balances and income sustainability may be overstated. This model cannot calculate the taxable fund earnings required for that tax.
  • Today's-dollar display discounts closing balances by the full elapsed years; opening balances by elapsed years at opening; annual flows by the mid-year factor. The nominal accounting identity therefore does not directly reconcile the individually discounted table columns. The 2.5% inflation assumption is the midpoint of the RBA's 2–3% target, not a guaranteed rate.
  • Constant returns do not model market volatility, sequencing risk or future rule changes. An unfunded-income year is more informative than an exact “money runs out” age when minimum or percentage withdrawals shrink with the balance.
